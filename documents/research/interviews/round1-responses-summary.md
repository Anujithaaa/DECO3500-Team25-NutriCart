How do budget, time, and convenience influence what you buy? 

**Kenan 1:** Time matters more than budget - rushing between classes means grabbing whatever's closest, even if it derails the planned list (swapped chicken/rice for frozen lasagna and instant noodles).



**Kenan 2:** Budget tracks how much he's been paid that week; describes himself as a "quick in-and-out" shopper who sticks to a pre-written list unless there's a sale worth a quick lap.

How do you decide what products to choose? 



**Kenan 1:** Price first, then familiarity/brand recognition from home. Doesn't read labels closely - picked a pricier "Greek style" yoghurt mainly because "high protein" was printed on the front.

**Kenan 2:** More label-driven - checks the nutrition panel for protein, fat, and sodium rather than brand. Doesn't bother comparing similar products once he's confirmed it's "basically the same food," just buys the cheaper version.

Ever thought "this is the healthy option" while shopping?  

**Kenan 1:** Picked a wrap over a pie because it "wasn't deep fried" and had visible veg - a vibe-based call, no nutrition check.

**Kenan 2:** Wasn't directly asked this in the same way, but consistently frames his own choices around chasing protein/low fat/low sodium rather than a single standout moment.

Anyone ever surprised you by calling something "healthy"?  

**Kenan 1:** A classmate's protein bars, marketed as a meal replacement, turned out to have more sugar than a can of soft drink.

**Kenan 2:** Says this hasn't really happened to him - he does his own research before trusting a "healthy" label on a recipe he finds online.

First reaction to the NutriCart prototype  

**Kenan 1:** Read it immediately as a social layer on top of grocery shopping "like if Strava and a shopping list app had a baby."

**Kenan 2:** Also drew the Strava comparison unprompted, but for the friends/recommendation feature specifically - "seeing your friends' running stats" vs. seeing what they're eating.

Friend recommendations - want to see them in-store or beforehand? 

**Kenan 1:** Liked seeing it in-store - concrete ingredient list to grab right there.



**Kenan 2:** Preferred seeing it before leaving home, as discovery/inspiration while planning, not mid-shop.

Whose recommendation would you trust?  

**Kenan 1:** Only people with similar constraints/eating habits - not "anyone," and not people with very different budgets or kitchens.

**Kenan 2:** Similar - treats it as inspiration rather than something he'd commit to buying off; wants it to be people he's actually added as friends.

Group goal / progress feature (2/3, 3/3, 1/3)  

**Kenan 1:** Mildly motivating as-is, but said a bigger leaderboard-style version would tip into pressure.

**Kenan 2:** Flagged the budget angle specifically - seeing a friend's "premium" ingredients could create insecurity for someone who can't afford the same products, though he leans toward it being mostly positive as accountability.

Timing of in-store suggestions  

**Kenan 1:** Good moment for the "one more dinner to reach your goal" nudge; wants the friends' overview earlier, before leaving home.

**Kenan 2:** Same instinct - wants the group/social content front-loaded before the shop, not competing for attention mid-store.

Would you use it?  

**Kenan 1:** Yes, but selectively - keeps the recommendations/shopping-list side, would ignore the group-comparison side.

**Kenan 2:** Yes, and more enthusiastically - his one condition is adding prices next to ingredients, which he said would actually save him time versus checking prices himself; also wants clarity on whether it's a diet-tracking app or a grocery-list app, since right now it reads as both.
