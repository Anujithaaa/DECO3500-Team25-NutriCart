## Notes

### Q1: Walk me through your last grocery trip – which store near campus did you go to, how did you get there, how did you decide what to buy, and what actually ended up in your basket?

I went to Coles because it was convenient for me and I didn't want to travel too far. I usually make a list on my phone before I go, especially if I have assignments or work coming up. I bought the basic things I needed for the week, like chicken, vegetables, bread, eggs and some fruit. But I also bought a couple of snacks because I saw them while walking through the aisles. So even though I have a list, I don't always stick completely to it.

### Q2: Tell me about a time when a friend, housemate, or someone you live with changed what you bought or cooked. What happened and how did their input affect your choices at the shops?

My friends definitely influence my food choices. If we're planning to eat together, we normally decide what we're going to cook before going shopping. There have also been times when a friend recommended a cheaper brand or a particular product, and I've bought that instead of what I originally planned. I think it's easier to try something when someone you know has already recommended it.

### Q3: Think about some food content you've seen recently on social media, like a recipe, trend, or someone's meal. Has anything you've seen ever influenced what you bought on your next grocery trip? Walk me through what happened.

Yes. I regularly see food videos on TikTok and Instagram, especially quick recipes that don't require too many ingredients. Sometimes I'll save them and try them during the week. One time I saw a really simple pasta recipe and thought it looked easy, so when I went shopping I bought the ingredients specifically to make it. I probably wouldn't have thought about making that meal if I hadn't seen it online.

### Q4: Describe a time when you were shopping and had to choose between a healthier option and something cheaper or more convenient. What were you thinking about, and what did you end up choosing?

This happens quite often. If I have enough money available, I normally prefer the healthier option, but when I'm trying to stay within my budget I'll look for something cheaper. I also compare different brands because sometimes the cheaper option isn't necessarily less healthy. If I can find something that's reasonably healthy and on special, that's usually what I'll choose.

### Q5: What's the hardest part about eating well as a student living near UQ? Can you give me a recent example?

For me, it's mainly time and money. When I have several assignments due, grocery shopping and cooking become less of a priority. It's tempting to just buy something ready-made because it's faster. The stores closer to UQ are convenient, but I feel like I can get better prices if I travel further away. So sometimes I have to choose between convenience and saving money.

### Closing: Is there anything else about your grocery shopping experience that you think I should know about?

I think social media gives students lots of ideas about healthy food, but seeing an idea online doesn't necessarily mean you'll actually buy it. Once you're in the supermarket, the price, your budget, what's on special and how much time you have can change your decision.
