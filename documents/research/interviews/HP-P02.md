## Notes

*[interviewer] - Second person (interviewed before however its the first interview)*

- Said they’ll use the shopping list the most, they already have a shared list via SMS. Having it on the cart unit will be really helpful, “I don’t have to remember what my roommate texted me a few days ago”
  - “Watching the running total update is satisfying, kind of like a mini dopamine hit, and it stops me from doing that thing where I get to the checkout and I'm $15 over what I meant to spend.”
  - Asked what happens if they grab something thats NOT on the list, what happens then? “If there was a sale on avocados will it let me add them or have to add to home dock to work?”
- Liked that compare screen “doesn't tell me which one is "healthier" or "better"”
  - Used health apps before that made it too preachy/judgy
  - “am I standing in the aisle scanning two cans of peas while people wait to get past me? If scanning two items takes more than like 10 seconds each, I'm just going to grab the cheaper one and move on.”
    - How fast the scan and compare flow is?
- On the fence about the housemate suggestions and alerts.
  - The idea of letting people know which grocery store has the cheaper price is really useful. But wants it to be rarer and not popup a lot.
    - “If every second aisle my cart is buzzing with a housemate's opinion, that's going to feel like noise, not help, and I'll start ignoring it entirely”
    - Same like ignoring notifications they added
    - The “already at home” and deals alerts are the most valuable since its “money saving”.
