## Notes

### Q1: Walk me through your last grocery trip – which store near campus did you go to, how did you get there, how did you decide what to buy, and what actually ended up in your basket?

My last grocery trip was to Woolworths at Indooroopilly. I usually go there because there are more options compared to the smaller stores around UQ. I went by bus, and before going I had a rough idea of what I needed because I was planning meals for the next few days. I mainly bought rice, chicken, vegetables, eggs and some snacks. I also ended up buying a few things that weren't on my list because they were on special.

### Q2: Tell me about a time when a friend, housemate, or someone you live with changed what you bought or cooked. What happened and how did their input affect your choices at the shops?

Yeah, definitely. When I shop with my friends, they influence what I buy quite a lot. Sometimes we plan to cook something together, so instead of buying separate meals, we'll buy ingredients that everyone can share. My friends also point out cheaper brands or products that they've tried before. If someone says a particular product is good and cheaper, I'm more likely to give it a try.

### Q3: Think about some food content you've seen recently on social media, like a recipe, trend, or someone's meal. Has anything you've seen ever influenced what you bought on your next grocery trip? Walk me through what happened.

Yes, I've definitely been influenced by Instagram and TikTok. I see a lot of quick meal and meal-prep videos. If something looks easy enough to make, I'll sometimes save the video and buy the ingredients later. For example, I've seen different chicken and rice meal-prep recipes online and then bought similar ingredients when I went shopping. Social media is useful because sometimes I don't know what to cook, so it gives me ideas.

### Q4: Describe a time when you were shopping and had to choose between a healthier option and something cheaper or more convenient. What were you thinking about, and what did you end up choosing?

Price is usually one of the biggest things I consider. If the healthier option is only slightly more expensive, I'll probably choose it. But if there is a big difference in price, I'll usually choose the cheaper one. When I'm busy with university, convenience also becomes important. Sometimes I'll buy something that's quick to prepare rather than spending a lot of time cooking, even if I know there is a healthier alternative.

### Q5: What's the hardest part about eating well as a student living near UQ? Can you give me a recent example?

I think the hardest part is balancing cost, time and convenience. As a student, you have assignments and other commitments, so cooking every meal isn't always realistic. Groceries can also become expensive, especially when you're trying to buy fresh food regularly. Sometimes it's easier to buy something around campus because you don't have time to go grocery shopping or cook. So for me, eating well isn't necessarily about knowing what is healthy; it's more about actually being able to afford it and having enough time to prepare it.

### Closing: Is there anything else about your grocery shopping experience that you think I should know about?

I think having an easier way to decide what to buy while actually being in the supermarket would be useful. Sometimes you know you want to eat healthier, but once you're in the store there are so many choices and prices that you just choose whatever is easiest or cheapest.
