**Interviewer:** Thank you so much for doing this interview. Before I start I just want to cover a few things, this is part of a university project, we're interviewing students about grocery shopping and you'll get to try out one of our early app prototypes as well. Are you good with being recorded? This is being recorded, it's not going to be shared, it's only going to be between the group members and we're purely going to use it for data. Cool, so I'll just start with the first question. Do you shop with other people usually?

**Participant:** Not really, mostly by myself actually. My flatmate and I have completely different schedules, she's got labs most afternoons and I've got a part time job on weekends, so we just don't really sync up. Sometimes if she's free we'll walk down together but even then we kind of split up once we're inside and just message each other when we're done.

**Interviewer:** Yeah that's fair. Can you tell me about the last time you shopped with someone else, like where were you and is there a specific item you picked up or put back because they were there?

**Participant:** Okay so last time was maybe three weeks ago at the Coles near my accommodation, she grabbed a tub of protein powder and I remember going wait you actually use that, and she was like yeah I have a shake after gym, and I ended up grabbing a small one too just to try it because she made it sound easy. So I guess that's an item I picked up because she was there, if I'd been alone I probably wouldn't have even walked down that aisle.

**Interviewer:** And how does budget, time and convenience influence what you buy?

**Participant:** Honestly time is the bigger one for me over budget, though budget still matters, if I'm rushing between classes I basically go for whatever's closest to the entrance or whatever I can throw together without thinking.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time you were in a rush or tight on money at the shops, what did you end up grabbing instead of what you planned, and why?

**Participant:** Last week I had a nine am and then had to be back on campus by one, so I basically sprinted through Woolies. I had a list, chicken, rice, veggies for stir fry, but I ended up grabbing a frozen lasagna and some instant noodles instead because they were right there near checkout and I didn't want to go find the fresh chicken at the back of the store. Cheaper too, so it kind of ticked both boxes even though it wasn't the plan.

**Interviewer:** And how do you decide on what products to choose when grocery shopping?

**Participant:** Price first if I'm honest, then whatever's familiar, like brands my mum used to buy back home, or stuff I've had before and know I like. I don't really read nutrition labels properly, I just glance at the front of the pack.

**Interviewer:** So do you usually compare two similar products before deciding then?

**Participant:** Yeah, actually two weeks ago I was stuck between two yoghurt tubs, a cheaper generic brand and a slightly pricier Greek style one. I ended up going with the Greek one even though it was like two dollars more, because it said high protein on the front and I figured that's a better deal for the price per gram of protein or whatever, even though I didn't actually check.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time you specifically thought this is the healthy option while grocery shopping, what was the item, what made you call it healthy, and what did you compare it against?

**Participant:** Probably grabbing a wrap instead of a meat pie from the hot food section. I called the wrap healthy mainly because it had visible lettuce and tomato in it and wasn't deep fried, compared to the pie which is obviously all pastry and mince. I didn't check calories or anything, it was just a vibe, less greasy equals healthier.

**Interviewer:** Has anyone, like a friend, family member, or something you saw online, ever told you something was healthy that surprised you or that you disagreed with?

**Participant:** Yeah actually, a guy on my course swears by those protein bars, says they're basically a meal replacement and so much healthier than a sandwich. I looked at the back of one once and the sugar content was kind of wild, like more than a can of soft drink almost. I didn't say anything to him but I remember being surprised, because it's marketed with all this high protein low carb stuff on the front that made it look really clean.

**Interviewer:** Alright, so the next part of this interview, I sent you a link that leads to our early app prototype. I want you to open it up and just explore the app a bit and say your thoughts out loud.

**Participant:** Okay this is pretty cool. It says demo up here, you're at Woolworths Toowong, want a little help deciding what to buy. Okay so home screen, I see NutriCart, it's picked up that I'm at Woolworths Toowong, there's a friends recommend card and a group goal card about cooking three healthier dinners this week. Before you explain anything, my gut read is it's like a social app layered on top of grocery shopping, kind of like if Strava and a shopping list app had a baby, it seems to be nudging me toward healthier choices by showing me what my friends are eating rather than just giving me generic tips.

**Interviewer:** So for the friend recommendation feature, would you actually want to see a friend's saved meal, like the burrito bowl, while you're standing in the store?

**Participant:** Honestly, yeah kind of, seeing Sarah's burrito bowl with an actual ingredient list while I'm standing in the aisle is more useful than I expected, it's concrete, I can just grab the exact things listed instead of standing there trying to think of a recipe from scratch. That part I like.

**Interviewer:** And whose recommendation would you trust enough to act on, anyone or only specific people?

**Participant:** Definitely not anyone. I'd trust it if it's someone I actually know eats similarly to me, or has similar constraints, like another broke uni student, not some random with a huge kitchen and unlimited time. If it's a friend who I know cooks properly, sure, random acquaintance, probably wouldn't even click on it.

**Interviewer:** And just on the group goal or progress feature, how do you feel seeing your progress next to your friends' progress, like two of three, three of three, one of three?

**Participant:** Seeing two of three next to Sarah's three of three, not gonna lie, a little bit of a damn she's ahead of me feeling, it's not terrible though, it's mild enough that it feels more like a nudge than a scoreboard.

**Interviewer:** Would this motivate you or would it feel like pressure or comparison you'd rather not see?

**Participant:** If it were more prominent, like a leaderboard with everyone ranked, I think that'd start feeling like pressure instead of motivation, as it is, small card, soft numbers, it leans more toward motivating for me, but I could see it stressing someone else out.

**Interviewer:** This suggestion popped up while you were in store, is that the right moment or would you rather see this before you leave home, or at a different point in the shop?

**Participant:** Showing one more dinner to reach your goal while I'm literally in the store is actually the right moment for that one, because it's actionable right then, I can go grab the missing ingredient. But I feel like the group goal overview itself, with everyone's numbers side by side, I'd rather see that before I leave home, like as a heads up, not while I'm mid shop trying to focus on my list.

**Interviewer:** And this last question, I want you to be completely honest with me, would you actually use this app?

**Participant:** Instead of a flat yes or no, I think I'd use it, but only for the friend recommendation and shopping list parts, not really for the group goal or social comparison part. I'd keep it open for what should I grab today, the way I'd check a recipe on my phone, but I'd probably turn off or ignore the progress tracking against friends bit, because that feels more like a nice to have than something I need, it's the what do I actually buy right now problem that's the one costing me time and stress.

**Interviewer:** That's all the questions I have for you, thank you so much for your time.

**Participant:** No worries, thank you for having me.
