SLIDE 2 — Problem Space (2:00) [Anujitha presents]: "Our domain statement: We are interested in enhancing healthy food choices for university students who live and shop near the UQ St Lucia campus, taking place when grocery shopping in person, with the possibilities of social and contextual computing. Our review of the literature revealed three critical gaps in this space. First — Marshall et al.'s 2022 survey of food choice technology found that nearly all existing tools focus on individual preferences like taste and nutrition tracking. Social factors — peer influence, shared goals, what your friends eat — are largely ignored in food technology design. Second — Wang et al.'s 2024 study published in CSCW showed that social media food content inspires people to want to eat differently, but this inspiration rarely translates into what people actually buy. There's a clear disconnect between scrolling at home and standing in the supermarket aisle. Third — while in-store tools like Strecker et al.'s ShoppingCoach can support individual healthier choices, they have no social dimension. And Di Cosola and Neff showed that social comparison nudges do work for healthier purchasing — but only in online grocery contexts. Nobody has tested them in physical stores. The gap is clear: no existing system combines social features with context-aware support at the point of in-store grocery purchase for university students."

SLIDE 4 — Design Concepts (2:00–2:30) [James presents]: "Based on our research and interviews, we've identified our design opportunity: How might context-aware mobile technology, combined with social features, support UQ students in making healthier food choices while grocery shopping near campus? It shows what your friends and housemates bought this week, shared budget-friendly healthy recipes, and your group's health goals.
